Study 002 · A closer look

The birth of a loop

A sharp tip is a balancing act. Keep Needle’s rhythms at 8 and 21, and change just the length of its innermost arm. A little loop appears on one side of the balance; a rounded turn on the other.

65 · A moment at rest

The motions balance exactly. The point stops at the tip and turns back along a cusp.

Why does the loop appear?

At the tip, the two forward-turning arms contribute 245 + 140 × 8 = 1,365. The backward-turning arm contributes 21 × its length. These are velocities measured against the first arm’s angle.

Below 65, the forward motion wins and rounds the turn. At 65, the velocities cancel for an instant. Above 65, the backward motion wins at the tip. The point turns back on itself, crossing its own earlier path and enclosing a little loop.

The cusp reference is aligned at the tip so the change is easier to see. This lens shows a short interval on either side of that tip, rather than the full orbit.
