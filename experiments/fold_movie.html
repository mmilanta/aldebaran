Two counts tell different stories

A touch changes the number of crossing pairs: 7 → 21 → 35 for seven folds, 70 → 98 → 126 for fourteen. A triple leaves those pairs in place and changes their order.

Strangeness, abbreviated St, remembers that order. Positive triples add one at each meeting point; negative triples subtract one. For fourteen folds it rises from 14 to 28, returns to 14, then rises to 28 again. The number of pairs stays at 126 during that last down-and-up.

The lines show values between event phases. At a touch, the tangent pairs give a pair count halfway between the neighboring plateaux. Strangeness is shown only for regular drawings between the events.

No room for another meeting

The earlier calculation found every touch and proved how many crossing pairs exist between them. Small moving charts now account for every one of those pairs across closed phase intervals. Checking which charts can share visits rules out any additional triple.

The result is exact: seven positive triples for seven folds; twenty-eight positive and fourteen negative for fourteen. The half-turn endpoints have freshly certified crossing words too. The result concerns these two families and their fixed arm lengths.
