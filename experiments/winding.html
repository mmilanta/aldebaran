How can an area be negative?

Ordinary painted area counts every painted patch once. Signed area remembers how the curve travels: a region with count +2 contributes twice its area, while a region with count −1 subtracts its area. A region with count zero contributes nothing.

For these three arms, the exact total is π × (245² + 140² × outer rhythm − 65² × inner rhythm). The squared lengths give area; the rhythms give how many times each arm turns. Mixed terms cancel over a full cycle.

Try “A negative total.” Its backward loops outweigh its positive regions, yet the center still counts as +1. In fact, every point less than 40 units from the center counts as +1 in both drawings. The dotted circle marks that sheltered disk.

What exactly am I seeing?

These are the sketchbook’s curves reflected vertically so that positive turns run counterclockwise, as on a mathematical page. Their shapes and arm lengths are the same. Nothing moves until you change a control.

A count describes the curve’s total turning around the chosen point, with opposite turns canceling. It stays the same throughout a connected region and changes only when the point crosses the curve. Directly on the line, the count is undefined; near a boundary, move the point a little farther away.

The picture uses a finely sampled polygon, so very small regions may be hard to see. The count buttons visit examples, including thin overlaps. Away from the line, the point’s count is checked against a bound on the gap between the polygon and the smooth curve. The signed area above comes from the exact formula.
